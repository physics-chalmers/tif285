Oral exam — instructions

The oral exam concerns the two projects and takes place in the exam week. You are examined individually: you draw three questions from a published question bank, prepare on your own for at least 30 minutes, and then have a discussion of about 10 minutes with the examiner. A teaching assistant (TA) is present throughout. You are told your provisional points before you leave. The exam is held in English.

Signing up and arriving

Book a slot in Canvas; slots are first come, first served. At your booked time, wait in the corridor outside the exam rooms, five minutes early, and allow about one hour in total. A TA will meet you there. If you cannot attend, rebook in Canvas if a place is free, or email the examiner as early as you can. If you have a decision granting examination support, email the examiner at least two weeks before the exam week.

The questions

The question bank is published on Canvas at least two weeks before the exam. Your envelope contains three questions from it:

Points
Q1 on your preferred project 0–6
Q2 on the other project 0–6
Q3 an extension question 0–6 or 0–8

You name your preferred project when you are welcomed, before you draw the envelope. It decides only which project is discussed first. For each project the bank has technical questions (reconstruct a method, get the details right) and evaluative questions (take a position on the analysis and defend it); your envelope holds one of each, so prepare both kinds for both projects. The extension question asks about a situation the projects did not cover: “what would change if…”, “why would this fail when…”.

The optional extra tasks are examined only through Q3. It comes in three variants, labelled on the question card: (a) for students who did no extra task, worth 0–6 points, and (b) or (c) for students who did the Project 1 or the Project 2 extra task, worth 0–8. When you are welcomed you say which extra tasks you did. If you did both, the card tells you which variant is yours, so prepare both. The grade thresholds are the same for everyone.

On the day

  1. Welcome. ID check. You name your preferred project and your extra tasks, draw a sealed envelope, and the TA marks your Q3 variant. Bags and phones go on a shelf at the door; bring nothing but a pen.
  2. Preparation (at least 30 min). A quiet room, up to three students at separate desks, with a TA who can clarify what a question means but not the answer. On the desk: printed copies of both project descriptions, note paper, and a handover card for the examiner. The card asks, among other things, one question everybody answers in writing: Did you use generative AI tools in the projects? If so, for what, and how did you check the output? AI assistance is allowed under the course policy; what is examined is that you understand what you handed in.
  3. Discussion (about 10 min). With the examiner and the TA, one question after the other. Bring your notes and refer to them.
  4. Result. You wait outside briefly while the examiner and the TA confer, then you are told your provisional points.

Points and grades

Oral exam points
12 or more required for the highest grade
10 or more required for grade 4 / VG
6 or more required to pass

The points stated at the end of your exam are provisional; the grade decision is taken once everyone has been examined and is reported in Ladok. If your result is close to the pass threshold you are told that it goes to review instead of a number. Questions about the assessment go to the examiner by email after results are published, not at the door.

How to prepare

Passing marks come from a correct and coherent account of what was done, how, and with what result. Higher marks come from what is not necessarily in your notebook: why a choice was made, what it assumes, where the result is weakest, and what would change under other circumstances. Work through the question bank for both projects with that in mind. Expect to be interrupted and asked “why”: prepare to be questioned, not to recite. “I don’t know, but here is how I would find out” is a good answer. Discussing the questions with other students is allowed and useful.
